Questions, answered

Cap table basics.

What is a cap table?

A capitalization table — a "cap table" — is the record of who owns what in a company: every share, option, SAFE, and convertible note, and the ownership percentage each represents on a fully diluted basis. It's the source of truth for fundraising, employee equity, and any capital event.

What is cap table software?

Cap table software replaces error-prone spreadsheets with a live, reconciled ledger of every security class. It tracks issuances, transfers, conversions, and option grants; keeps a full version history and audit trail; and produces investor-ready exports on demand.

When does a startup need cap table software?

The moment ownership stops being trivial — after your first priced round, your first SAFEs or convertible notes, or your first option grants. Once more than a couple of stakeholders are involved, a spreadsheet drifts out of sync and becomes a liability during diligence.

How should an early-stage startup choose cap table software?

Look for one reconciled ledger with version history, an audit trail, and every issuance tied to its agreement. Every security class (common, preferred, SAFEs, convertible notes, options, and warrants) should be modeled explicitly, including conversions, so fully diluted ownership stays true before and after a round. Ask whether the tool also covers scenario modeling, financial evidence, and diligence sharing, and whether the provider publishes current pricing you can verify. Diligence Ready's Founders plan includes it free for 3 users and 1 scenario. To compare tools, see alternatives.

Can I import my existing cap table from a spreadsheet?

Yes. Bring your existing cap table in from a spreadsheet, and Diligence Ready reconciles imported numbers onto one ledger on the way in — you remain responsible for the records — so the migrated version is one you can defend in diligence.

Does Diligence Ready handle SAFEs and convertible notes?

Yes. SAFEs, convertible notes, warrants, options, and common and preferred stock are all first-class security types. Conversions are modeled explicitly, so your fully diluted ownership stays accurate before and after a round.
