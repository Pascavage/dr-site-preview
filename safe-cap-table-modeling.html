When scenario modeling helps

Before you decide, not after.

Founders reviewing an incoming SAFE, note, or term sheet can see the ownership effect before signing anything. CFOs, advisors, and accountants can walk a client through the same modeled outcome, on assumptions the client controls, before anything is recorded.

What you can model

Assumptions in, an ownership outcome out.

Based on the terms you enter for the instrument you're evaluating, not a legal reading of the agreement itself.

①

Assumptions

The terms you enter for a SAFE, note, share issuance, or funding round.

②

Conversion or round scenario

How those terms play out at a modeled priced round or issuance date.

③

Ownership effect

The resulting stake for each holder, shown as a modeled outcome.

Scenario, then review, then record

A modeled outcome is not a committed transaction.

The assumptions and the modeled outcome are labeled separately from anything committed to your cap table. Nothing here supports every SAFE or note variant, and none of it is a legal interpretation of your specific agreement. Review the modeled result, then decide separately whether to record a transaction.
