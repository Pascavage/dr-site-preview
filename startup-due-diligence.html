Why one folder isn't enough

No two requests ask for exactly the same thing.

An investor, an acquirer, and a lender each ask for a different mix of documents, and the list changes by deal type. Request lists vary; this reflects common categories, not a fixed or legally complete list for any specific request.

Organizing evidence by category ahead of time means most of any given request is already covered.

What diligence covers

Six categories, organized on an ongoing basis.

①

Ownership

Cap table, security agreements, and equity grants, reconciled and current.

②

Financial

Statements and financial evidence tracked on a reporting cadence, not assembled once.

③

Governance

Board minutes, consents, and corporate records kept in one organized place.

④

Legal

Material contracts and agreements organized alongside the rest of the record.

⑤

IP

Assignments and registrations tracked so open items are visible before they're asked about.

⑥

Go-to-market

Customer and pipeline evidence organized alongside the rest of the diligence record.

Status & gaps, not just files

See what's covered and what's still open.

Each category shows status against a checklist, so gaps are visible on an ordinary day, not discovered mid-request. This is a workflow for maintaining evidence, not only a place to upload files.
