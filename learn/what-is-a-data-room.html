What is a data room, and why do investors ask for one?

A data room is a secure, organized place where a company shares confidential documents with investors, buyers or lenders during due diligence. Investors ask for one because it lets them check what you’ve told them against the underlying documents in one place, with access you control and a record of who viewed what.

Plain-English guide · Updated October 2026

When you raise a round, the investor’s team needs to review your documents: incorporation papers, the cap table, financials, key contracts, IP assignments and more. A data room is where you share them. When it runs online it is often called a virtual data room, or VDR. It is organized by category, access-controlled per person and logged, instead of scattered across email attachments.

Key facts

  • When you need one: a fundraise, an acquisition, a loan or an audit.
  • Who sets it up: the company. The founder, CEO or CFO usually owns it, often with help from a Trusted Advisor such as a fractional CFO, accountant or counsel.
  • Investor data room: the data room you open for investors during a round. A VC data room is the same thing, named for venture capital investors.
  • Data room vs shared drive: a shared drive stores and shares files; a purpose-built data room adds a diligence workflow and may provide more specialized review controls.
  • How it’s priced: models vary. Some data rooms charge by page or document volume, others by user or by plan. Our data room pricing guide compares the models.

Why investors ask for a data room

An investor’s decision rests on what you’ve told them: who owns what, how the numbers add up, which contracts matter, and whether the company owns what it builds. Diligence is where they check each of those against the documents behind it. A data room lets them do that in one place.

  • One organized index. Reviewers find each document by category instead of searching email threads.
  • The version that counts. Use executed agreements where signatures are required, plus current, clearly dated records. Label requested drafts and outstanding signatures separately.
  • Controlled access. Each investor, and their counsel, sees only what you’ve shared with them.
  • A record. You can see who opened what, so you know where the review stands.

Every missing or hard-to-find document becomes a follow-up request, and a review that keeps stalling puts momentum at risk. An organized room lets the review move at the pace of the investor’s questions, not your search for files.

When investors ask for one

Usually once an investor is seriously considering the round, often around the term sheet. Earlier rounds may need a shorter list. Later rounds and acquisitions usually go deeper. Keeping the room current between rounds means sharing it is a permission, not a project.

Review sensitive records with the appropriate advisor before sharing. Redact unnecessary personal or account details and restrict access to payroll, employee disputes, bank details and potentially privileged material.

What goes in an investor data room

A common starting index uses five categories (illustrative, not exhaustive; acquisitions may require more categories and specialist review):

  1. Corporate & formation. Certificate of incorporation, bylaws, board consents and board minutes.
  2. Cap table & equity. The fully diluted cap table, option grants and vesting, SAFEs and convertible notes.
  3. Financials & tax. Financial statements, tax returns, and supporting schedules such as AR aging and bank statements. See financial reporting for startups.
  4. Material contracts. The key customer, supplier and other agreements the company depends on.
  5. IP assignments. Assignment agreements, registrations and other proof the company owns what it builds.

For the item-by-item list, use the data room checklist in the due diligence checklist.

Data room vs pitch deck vs shared folder

  • Pitch deck: the story you tell to win the meeting. It summarizes; it doesn’t prove.
  • Data room: the documents behind the story, for the investors who want to check it.
  • Shared folder: general-purpose file sharing. Services such as Google Drive support named access and revocation, but available logging, watermarking and download controls vary by product, plan and configuration. Compare the controls you actually need rather than assuming any shared folder is either sufficient or insecure.

Keeping control of what you share

The protections that matter most in practice:

  • Individual invitations with email-code verification, so possessing the link alone is not enough to gain access
  • Identity watermarking on supported document types; Diligence Ready applies it to guest PDFs
  • Download controls: a room-wide download setting with per-guest overrides
  • Revocation that blocks subsequent access requests. Previously downloaded copies cannot be recalled.
  • An access log of who opened which document, and when

How to set up a data room

In short: decide where the files will live, build a folder for each of the five categories, add executed agreements and current, dated records with one naming pattern, give each reviewer their own access, and check what’s missing before you invite anyone. The step-by-step guide is how to set up a due diligence data room.

How should an early-stage startup choose a data room for a seed round?

Look for a data room that gives each investor their own access, watermarks the document formats you plan to share, logs who opened what, and publishes pricing you can verify.

  • Pricing model: some providers charge by page or document volume. Others charge by user or by plan.
  • Per-guest access: individual invitations with email-code verification.
  • Downloads: check the room-wide setting and per-guest overrides.
  • Room to grow: check what the next plan includes before you need it.

FAQ

What is a data room for investors?

It is the secure, organized place where a startup shares the documents investors review before they invest: incorporation papers, the cap table, financials, key contracts and IP assignments. Each investor is invited individually, and guest file views and downloads are logged.

What is a VC data room?

It is the data room a startup opens for venture capital investors during a round. It often starts from the same five-category index as other diligence rooms: corporate and formation, cap table and equity, financials and tax, material contracts, and IP assignments.

What is the difference between a data room and a virtual data room?

A virtual data room is an online data room. Access permissions, watermarking, activity logs and download controls vary by provider and plan.

Why do investors want a data room instead of email attachments?

A data room gives them one organized place, current and clearly dated versions, their own access and a record of what they’ve reviewed. Email attachments make centralized permissions, version tracking and a shared review index harder to maintain.

Is a due diligence data room different from an investor data room?

Not in kind. Both share documents with the people reviewing a deal. An acquisition review usually goes deeper and involves more reviewers from more firms. For the setup steps, see how to set up a due diligence data room.

How much does a data room cost?

It depends on the pricing model. Some providers charge by page or document volume, so the bill can grow with the deal. Others charge by user or by plan. Diligence Ready includes 1 data room on the free Founders plan, with no per-page fees on any plan. Current plans are on the pricing page.

Can I use Google Drive as a data room?

You can share Drive folders directly. Services such as Google Drive support named access and revocation, but logging, watermarking and download controls vary by product, plan and configuration. Diligence Ready keeps your files in your Google Drive or OneDrive and adds individual guest invitations, identity watermarking on guest PDFs and an access log.

How long should a data room stay open?

Keep guest access open while the deal is under review. Revoke it when the deal closes or ends. Keep the room itself current afterward, so the next round or audit starts from an organized record.

Key takeaways

  • A data room is where you share diligence documents. It is organized, access-controlled and logged.
  • Investors use it to check what you have told them against the underlying documents.
  • Start from an illustrative five-category index so reviewers find things fast.
  • Individual invitations, emailed access codes, PDF watermarking and an access log help you keep control.
