Buy-side due diligence: what a clean room looks like

An organized index, documents tied to records, gated access, and an audit trail — so reviewers are not hunting a folder dump.

Plain-English guide · Updated October 2026

Reviewers see an organized index, not a folder dump. Each item is current, linked to its underlying record, and visible only to the people you've granted access — with every action logged.

Buy-side vs sell-side

Buy-side due diligence is the reviewer's job: read the evidence behind ownership, financials, contracts, and IP. Sell-side is keeping that evidence ready before anyone asks. Same room. Different chair.

What belongs in the room

A data room — sometimes called a virtual data room or diligence room — is the secure, organized place where a company shares the documents an investor, acquirer, or lender needs during due diligence: incorporation papers, the cap table, financials, contracts, and IP assignments. Virtual data room costs depend on how the provider bills: by page, by user or by plan. This is what a diligence room looks like on Diligence Ready.

What buyers expect in a due diligence data room

Buyers work through five categories, and the question behind each request is simple: can you show it, and does it match? Expect requests for corporate and formation records, a fully diluted cap table tied to signed documents, financial statements and tax returns, material contracts, and IP assignments. The seller sets up the room and decides who gets in.

  • Corporate and formation: the charter, the bylaws, and the board consents behind major decisions.
  • Financials and tax: statements and tax returns, with the supporting schedules the buyer's accountants use to check them.
  • Material contracts: the customer, vendor, and partnership agreements that matter, so the buyer can see what the company has committed to.
  • An acquisition review usually asks for more history and more detail in every category than an investor in a round would. See the due diligence checklist, the sell-side guide, and what a data room is.

Controls buyers expect

  • Gated, role-based access for specific investors, advisors, or counsel
  • Personal link plus a one-time 6-digit code
  • Every document watermarked with the viewer's identity, server-side
  • Downloads are off by default
  • Audit trail on every change and every view

Documents tied to the ledger

Every file ties to the transaction or stakeholder it supports. A reviewer clicks from a line on the cap table straight to the signed agreement behind it.

Key takeaways

  • Buy-side reviews evidence; sell-side keeps it ready.
  • A clean room is an organized index with docs tied to records.
  • Gated access, watermarking, and an audit trail are live controls.
  • The cap table sits behind the documents reviewers click into.
